# Commercial Model Appendix

## 1. Purpose

This appendix makes the HermesOrbit business story more explicit for investor and operating discussions.

It does not claim forecast precision. It defines the commercial logic, the monetized value, and the validation milestones required before confidence should increase.

## 2. Commercial thesis

HermesOrbit monetizes a high-intent workflow, not general curiosity.

The product is valuable when it saves time, reduces missed interventions, increases trust in automation, and gives users a reason to keep real agent workflows running.

That means the monetizable value is:
- control
- continuity
- visibility
- secure mobile operations
- reduced downtime and decision latency

## 3. Wedge market definition

Initial wedge market:
- self-hosted Hermes users
- adjacent self-hosted agent users who could migrate
- technical founders and engineers already running long-lived agent workflows
- users comfortable paying for tools that increase leverage rather than entertainment

This is not the full agent market. It is a narrower, more monetizable segment.

## 4. TAM / SAM / SOM framing

The numbers below are scenario ranges for planning, not diligence-grade claims.

### TAM — broad agent operations market
Broad market logic:
- global agentic AI spend is projected by multiple public sources to grow into the tens of billions by 2030
- only a small slice of that is relevant to mobile control surfaces

Interpretation:
- TAM is large enough to make the category strategically interesting
- TAM is not the right primary proof for this business at current stage

### SAM — mobile operator tooling for agent power users
Serviceable addressable market should be framed around users who:
- run or supervise agents regularly
- use mobile intervention meaningfully
- care about self-hosting, privacy, or operator control

Planning view:
- likely in the **high tens of thousands to low hundreds of thousands** of globally addressable early adopters before mass hosted expansion
- enough for a premium tool business if conversion and retention are strong

### SOM — realistic first capture target
Near-term achievable market should be framed as:
- Hermes ecosystem users
- adjacent coding-agent / self-hosting communities
- technical operators in Telegram, Discord, X, GitHub, and indie-maker circles

Planning view:
- initial success does not require mass adoption
- a few thousand active high-intent users can validate pricing and hosted demand

## 5. Monetization sequence

## Stage 1 — self-hosted mobile Pro
Potential offer:
- free tier for launch-scope access with lighter limits and simpler defaults
- Pro subscription for power-user convenience, higher limits, and richer mobile operator workflows

Candidate Pro value:
- higher environment/instance limits
- advanced notification routing and richer approval ergonomics
- richer diagnostics and premium operator views
- widgets and shortcuts beyond the minimal launch layer
- broader history/retention and saved workflow conveniences

## Stage 2 — hosted plans
Potential offers:
- Hosted Solo
- Hosted Pro
- Hosted Team

Hosted value:
- no self-hosting burden
- managed infrastructure
- guided onboarding
- support and reliability guarantees
- integrated entitlements and account management

## Stage 3 — enterprise/team expansion
Potential value:
- shared environments
- RBAC
- audit exports
- policy controls
- team notifications and delegation

## 6. Candidate pricing logic

Commercial rule: `V1_SCOPE_DECISIONS.md` defines what ships in the launch product. Pricing tiers define what may be commercially gated, expanded, convenience-enhanced, or usage-limited on top of that launch scope.

These are strategic placeholders, not final launch pricing.

### Self-hosted Pro
Possible bands:
- monthly: `€8–€15`
- annual: `€79–€129`

Rationale:
- above casual-app pricing
- below dev-tool budget friction for serious users
- consistent with premium operator-tool positioning

### Hosted Solo
Possible bands:
- monthly: `€19–€49`

Rationale:
- price anchored to saved setup burden, managed reliability, and convenience
- narrow enough to stay consistent with the current business-plan anchors

### Hosted Pro / Team
Possible bands:
- monthly: `€49–€149`

Rationale:
- higher tier justified by concurrency, retention, support, browser/tool access, and operator-team workflows
- still directional until usage and willingness-to-pay validation are real

## 7. Funnel logic

### Free to Pro conversion hypothesis
Users upgrade when they hit one or more of these:
- need higher instance/environment limits or richer fleet workflows
- need better notification routing and faster approval ergonomics
- need premium dashboards, diagnostics, or saved operator views
- rely on the product weekly rather than occasionally

### Pro to Hosted conversion hypothesis
Users upgrade when they hit one or more of these:
- want reliability without maintaining the backend
- want faster onboarding for new environments
- want account-level management rather than local pairing only
- want support and lower operational burden

## 8. Validation milestones

Commercial confidence should increase only after these proofs:

### Proof 1 — usage proof
- recurring weekly use for status checks, approvals, or interventions

### Proof 2 — willingness-to-pay proof
- measurable conversion into Pro features or paid beta access

### Proof 3 — hosted intent proof
- users explicitly ask for managed setup or migration path

### Proof 4 — retention proof
- users continue using the app after the novelty period because it became part of their workflow

## 9. Biggest commercial risks

- the wedge is valuable but smaller than expected
- users may like the product but still prefer web for admin tasks
- hosted conversion may be weaker than self-hosted enthusiasm suggests
- pricing may need to be lower than ideal if differentiation is not obvious enough

## 10. Conclusion

The strongest commercial story is not “huge consumer AI app.”
It is:

> **a premium mobile operations product for serious agent users, with a clean expansion path into managed-hosted revenue.**

That is a narrower story, but it is more believable, more defensible, and easier to validate in stages.
