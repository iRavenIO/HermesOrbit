# HermesOrbit Business Plan

## 1. Business Thesis

HermesOrbit is not another AI chat app.

It is a premium mobile control product for running, monitoring, and managing Hermes Agent across self-hosted and future managed-hosted deployments.

The business opportunity comes from combining three trends:

1. more users want **agentic AI**, not just text chat
2. more power users are comfortable with **self-hosting and infra ownership**
3. there is still very weak mobile product coverage for **operating AI agents in production-like real workflows**

## 2. Problem Statement

Today, Hermes users can interact through CLI, messaging platforms, and the web dashboard. That is strong, but a gap remains:

- mobile access is fragmented
- mobile operations are not yet first-class
- approvals, job monitoring, and async interventions are underserved
- hosted future customers need a guided mobile experience, not raw infrastructure

The user pain is simple:

> People want their agents working when they are away from their desk, but they still need visibility, control, and trust from the phone in their pocket.

## 3. Why This Has Market Value

### 3.1 Strong category framing

The category is not “AI chat.”

The category is:

- mobile AI operations
- agent control plane
- pocket command center for AI workflows

That positioning is more defensible and more premium.

### 3.2 Clear differentiators

HermesOrbit can differentiate on:

- meaningful Hermes operator-workflow parity rather than toy chat
- multi-instance operator experience
- self-hosted credibility
- migration path to hosted service
- mobile-native approvals and push-driven operations

### 3.3 Strategic leverage

The app can drive value across multiple business layers:

- acquisition of self-hosted power users
- retention through daily operational use
- upsell into managed hosting
- long-term account hub for provisioning, billing, and fleet management

## 4. Target Segments

## 6. Revenue Strategy

## 6.1 Recommended business model sequence

### Phase 1 — paid app or freemium self-hosted tool

Two viable entry models:

#### Option A: premium paid app

- one-time purchase or annual subscription
- strongest quality signal
- simpler initial business model

#### Option B: freemium with paid pro features

Free:

- one instance
- limited history
- basic health and chat
- baseline push notifications and approval handling for core operator workflows
- lightweight access to the launch control plane with tighter limits on history, environments, and advanced convenience features

Pro:

- higher environment/instance limits and richer fleet workflows
- premium operator views and advanced diagnostics
- widgets and shortcuts beyond the minimal launch layer
- advanced notification routing and richer premium convenience flows
- broader history, retention, and power-user ergonomics

### Recommendation

Start with **freemium + Pro subscription** if managed hosting is a near-term goal.

Reason:

- lowers adoption friction
- creates recurring relationship
- clean path to attach hosted plans later

## 6.2 Apple monetization path

To keep the business plan executable on iOS, monetization must be designed with App Store mechanics in mind.

### Recommended interpretation

- **Self-hosted app features sold inside the iOS app** should assume App Store in-app purchase or subscription treatment.
- **Hosted Hermes service plans** can potentially be sold as an external service relationship, but the exact implementation should be reviewed against current App Store policy before launch.

### Practical recommendation

For the first release:

- keep the app commercially simple
- either use a clear in-app Pro subscription for mobile/operator features
- or ship free/low-cost while validating usage, then introduce hosted-plan monetization once the service layer is ready

### Avoid early ambiguity

Do not mix all of these in the first launch without legal/store review:

- paid unlock for self-hosted control features
- external web checkout for hosted plans
- hybrid account entitlements with unclear source of truth

A clean first commercial story is more important than maximizing monetization complexity.

## 6.3 Future hosted revenue

Once managed Hermes exists, offer:

- Hosted Solo
- Hosted Pro
- Hosted Team / Dedicated

Possible plan ladder:

- **Free / Local** — connect your own Hermes
- **Pro Mobile** — richer convenience, higher limits, and premium mobile operator workflows for self-hosted users
- **Hosted Solo** — one managed Hermes instance
- **Hosted Pro** — larger limits, more automations, better observability
- **Dedicated / Team** — enterprise-style deployment or team environment

## 7. Go-To-Market Strategy

## 7.1 Initial audience

Do not market to mass consumers first.

Start with:

- Hermes community and ecosystem users
- self-hosters
- makers
- indie hackers
- infra engineers
- agent workflow power users

These users already understand the problem and are easier to convert.

## 7.2 Launch channels

- Hermes ecosystem channels and docs
- X / Twitter demos
- short screen-recorded operational workflows
- Telegram/Discord communities
- Product Hunt / HN once product quality is strong
- “build in public” devlog around agent ops from mobile

## 7.3 Best demo angles

The best demo is not “look, chat works.”

The best demo is:

- start a job from phone
- receive push approval request
- inspect logs
- resume session
- change config or restart gateway
- confirm completion

That demonstrates real operational value.

## 8. Why App Store Publication Is Worth It

### Benefits

- credibility and discoverability
- habit loop via notifications and home screen presence
- lower-friction mobile access
- stronger premium feel than pure web access
- enables a future hosted business with an obvious customer touchpoint

### Risks

- branding/trademark ambiguity if public naming is too close to Hermes
- privacy review scrutiny
- complex remote-execution disclosure needs
- temptation to overbuild V1

### Mitigation

- use original public branding
- include explicit disclosure and privacy policy
- build meaningful native functionality
- keep first release focused on operator value

## 12. Risks and Responses

### Risk 1 — too technical for mainstream users

Response:

Start narrow. Win the power-user/operator niche first.

### Risk 2 — mobile parity is large in scope

Response:

Use a strict milestone plan while keeping MVP defined as parity-capable in blueprint terms.

### Risk 3 — App Store confusion around “Hermes” branding

Response:

Use an original public name and keep Hermes as a compatibility statement, not the primary brand.

### Risk 4 — hosted product arrives later than expected

Response:

Design the mobile architecture so self-hosted users still justify the product independently.

## 13. Recommendation

This project is worth doing if the goal is to create:

- a premium mobile operator product
- a conversion path into hosted Hermes
- a sticky daily-use surface around agent operations

The strongest business sequence is:

1. build self-hosted-first mobile control plane
2. grow among technical Hermes users
3. refine based on real ops workflows
4. launch managed-hosted tier
5. use the app as the front door and retention layer for the hosted business

Yes, the project has legitimate marketing and business value.

The value is strongest when marketed as:

- **AI agent operations from mobile**
- **your Hermes control plane in your pocket**
- **from self-hosted power tool to hosted premium platform**
